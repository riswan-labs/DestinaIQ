# Preliminary TAM, SAM and SOM

**Unit** is an annual travel planning occasion. A booking party can make multiple arrivals or multiple trips. The model keeps volume, purchaser share and product price separate.

| Layer | Reproducible calculation | Current interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM trip context | 1.52 billion 2025 international overnight arrivals (E01). Global domestic volume is unknown here; EU resident 2025 trips are an overlapping scoped series (E06). | International trip context is not a unique-user ceiling across both audiences. Qualified TAM = relevant international and domestic journeys × independent-planning share × problem-fit share, adjusted for deduplication. Those multipliers are unknown. Revenue TAM is unknown. |
| SAM traffic proxy | Initial feasibility candidates Japan 42.6836m + Singapore 16.9m + Maldives 2.246516m (E10, E11, E14) = about 61.8m inbound visits across the three source series. | Mixed methods and purposes, repeat and multi-country counting. This is gross candidate traffic. SAM = deduplicated qualified travel parties × supported places × language/region eligibility × reachable acquisition. Coverage and uptake inputs are unknown. |
| SOM | min(qualified acquisition × activation × conversion, delivery capacity) × realized net price. Count Trip Pass orders and retained subscriber-months separately to avoid counting upgrades twice. | Unknown until observed qualified traffic, trial behavior, eligibility, capacity, fulfillment costs and actual conversion exist. Stage a small pilot before estimating share. |

### Sensitivity without a forecast

For every 1,000 genuinely qualified and reached prospects, **one percentage point** of measured purchase conversion yields 10 paid orders. At the existing USD 9.99–19.99 gross Trip Pass hypothesis that would equal USD 99.90–199.90 before fees, refunds, tax, delivery and support. This is unit sensitivity, not a conversion assumption. At zero conversion the illustrative gross is zero. Capture power-user alert/API cost, refund exposure and subscriber churn at CP-0.4 and future commercialization gates.
