# Pricing sensitivity instrument

State that concepts are hypothetical and there is no sale, payment or promotion. First ask about actual prior tool purchases and cancellations, independently of the experiment.

- **Format split.** Randomly show a bounded trip pass or recurring monthly format first, with an identical explained feature bundle where feasible. Offer “keep my free tools”, “would not use it”, and “not enough information”. Ask which existing action it replaces and what would make it worth paying.
- **Gabor–Granger.** Use USD 9.99, 14.99 and 19.99 for Trip Pass. Rotate the starting point; ask about each distinct price at most once, raising after a yes and lowering after a no, and record the sequence. For recurring, test Plus USD 14.99/month and Pro USD 24.99/month with explicit feature boundaries and no claim of equal value.
- **Van Westendorp alternative.** For one clearly understood bundle ask when the price seems suspiciously cheap, a bargain, expensive but consider, or too expensive. Optional answers and check internal order. Avoid combining these responses with the other instrument as if the same.
- **Analysis.** Use the share of eligible respondents at each price who say they would consider it, list denominators, no-interest and uncertain. Cross-tab format by actual purchase history and trip frequency with privacy floors. Separate stated intent, observed points of abandonment, and future real behavior. Use no purchase button without separate approval.

CP-0.4 economics: source licensing minima, rate limits, alert refresh and sending, compute, human correction, support, fees, refund and free-tier subsidy. Compare per monitored trip and subscriber-month. No price decision.
